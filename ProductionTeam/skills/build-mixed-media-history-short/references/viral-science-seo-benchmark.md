# Viral Science Video and SEO Benchmark

Use this reference for a strange material, experiment, life hack, viral demonstration, or unexpected practical application.

## Episode contract

- Length: 25–35 seconds.
- Emotional curve: contradiction → curiosity → understanding → bigger surprise → joke.
- Silent-view goal: the impossible-looking result and subject name are understandable within 5–8 seconds.
- Evidence rule: AI video creates emotion; primary sources and Remotion graphics carry factual claims.
- Series rule: preserve the established badge, caption position, color system, and ending structure.

## Story pattern

1. **0–3 seconds — result first:** Show the strongest visual moment immediately. State one contradiction in large centered text.
2. **Name:** Show the exact subject name by roughly 5–8 seconds.
3. **Question:** Ask what it is for or why it happens. Briefly thin the BGM.
4. **Mechanism:** Explain one mechanism with a simple diagram rather than dense narration.
5. **Visible recovery:** Replay the source clip section that proves the initial contradiction.
6. **Unexpected application:** Switch to the second AI clip and clearly label it as an application visualization.
7. **Verified number:** Show one sourced number with its condition and institution in small text.
8. **Punchline:** Translate the scale change into a short human reaction, followed by the CTA.

Do not claim that a toy, viral product, or AI-demonstrated object is itself deployed in the application when only the same scientific principle is being researched. Say `同じ発想`, `同じ原理`, or `研究されています` as appropriate.

## Two Google Flow clips

### Clip 1: phenomenon

- Generate 8 seconds, vertical 9:16, one continuous action.
- Put the decisive event near 3.5–5.5 seconds so it can be used both as a cold open and later replay.
- Use a locked or slowly moving macro camera, stable geometry, realistic physics, and clean caption-safe space.
- End with a visually legible state change: dry grains, crystallization, spikes, breakage, melting, or another clear result.
- Exclude baked-in text, speech, music, logos, watermarks, extra hands, fantasy effects, and unrelated props.

### Clip 2: application or scale change

- Generate 8 seconds, vertical 9:16, one smooth reveal from detail to context.
- Begin with a close view of the application and pull back to show its real-world scale.
- Avoid scientific cutaways inside the generated video. Build diagrams in Remotion.
- Label the edited scene `AIによる応用イメージ`; never present it as field evidence.

Inspect start, middle, decisive frame, and end. Reject geometry changes, false text, implausible mechanics, unwanted logos, or a result that cannot be understood without narration.

## Remotion treatment

- Use the late section of clip 1 for the cold open, then rewind to its start for the name and setup.
- Reserve the strongest shake, ripple, flash, and impact sound for one decisive frame.
- Add a diagram only when it explains a relationship the AI video cannot prove.
- Display the number large; display conditions and source smaller but readable.
- Keep bottom captions centered and above Shorts UI.
- Use one semi-transparent central ending panel over the final footage. Center the subject label, punchline, divider, CTA, and channel name on one vertical axis.
- Keep source-video audio muted and rebuild narration, music, and effects deliberately.

## Voice gate

1. Store display text and spoken reading separately.
2. Write ambiguous words, proper names, and symbols phonetically in the reading field.
3. Generate VOICEVOX audio and measure every file.
4. Listen to all narration before final approval, especially homographs, place names, decimal numbers, percentages, and English loanwords.
5. If pronunciation is wrong, correct the reading rather than the visible kanji. Re-render audio, remux, normalize, and re-check.

## Search SEO gate

### Keyword discovery

- Read the channel's current traffic-source data. If Search is material, preserve searchable nouns even when optimizing for Shorts feed.
- Search current YouTube/web phrasing for the topic. Record recurring plain-language phrases and the exact technical term.
- Keep factual verification separate and use primary sources for claims.

### Title

Use this order:

`plain-language contradiction + exact subject + search intent + distinctive payoff + #Shorts`

Example:

`水に濡れない砂「マジックサンド」の仕組み｜砂漠農業の救世主!? #Shorts`

Place the highest-value phrase in the first half. Prefer one strong variant over keyword stuffing.

### Description

The first two lines must stand alone:

1. Ask the full search question using the plain-language phrase and exact subject.
2. Answer what the video explains, including the mechanism and unexpected application.

Then add qualification, primary sources, AI disclosure, voice/BGM credits, and exactly three focused public hashtags.

### Tags

Include only relevant variants:

- plain Japanese phrase;
- exact Japanese subject name;
- English subject name;
- mechanism;
- application;
- adjacent intent such as `仕組み`, `なぜ`, `科学実験`, or `自由研究`.

Tags supplement title and description; they do not replace a clear title.

## Publication and measurement

- Validate H.264/AAC, 1080×1920, 30 fps, 48 kHz stereo, duration, title length, description length, and tag count.
- Target about −16 LUFS and −1.0 to −1.5 dBTP.
- Set `containsSyntheticMedia: true` and `selfDeclaredMadeForKids: false` unless the specific video requires otherwise.
- Upload only after explicit authorization and re-query the scheduled list afterward.
- At 24 and 72 hours, record total views, Search traffic, actual search terms, Shorts traffic, viewed-vs-swiped, average percentage viewed, reactions, and subscriber conversion.
