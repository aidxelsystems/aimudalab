# Visual-first Joke Design

## One-frame contract

Define every candidate with:

```text
image_subject: the face, person, object, or system the eye finds first
image_scene: location, participants, action, and visible context
visual_wrongness: the single abnormal fact
composition: close/wide/overhead, subject position, background support
glance_test: why a silent one-second view communicates the setup
image_prompt: concrete vertical-generation instruction
```

Reject abstract emotions, backstory, invisible rules, or time sequences that cannot be shown in one frame.

## Visual palette across 14 frames

Mix:

- object close-up;
- wide room or venue;
- staff/customer interaction;
- reaction-focused portrait;
- machine, UI, receipt, menu, or sign;
- overhead or surveillance-like view;
- exaggerated crowd or duplication;
- impossible transformation or scale;
- a visually unique final tableau.

Change at least one of subject, setting, action, or camera distance between adjacent frames. Do not show the same counter-facing composition three times.

## AI-native visual boke

Use impossible but readable scenes: excessive scale, a venue behaving like another venue, too many staff, a machine with physical agency, mirrored worlds, literalized metaphors, or category-swapped equipment. Keep a familiar anchor from the topic so the viewer understands what is being violated.

## Image-first tsukkomi

Make the wrong object or role obvious in the image without printed explanation. Show the image alone, allow a short `?` beat, then reveal the flip and narration. A useful verbal frame is “〇〇かと思ったら、□□かよ”, but rotate phrasing outside deliberate tendon sequences.

## Prompt rules

- Request a clear vertical comedic scene.
- Name the subject, action, environment, visible wrongness, camera framing, and mood.
- Keep important faces and objects above the bottom 25-30%.
- Avoid relying on small or exact generated text.
- Avoid copyrighted characters and real celebrities.
- Ask for one dominant joke, not a collage of unrelated oddities.
- Vary scene texture across the set rather than repeating fourteen similar interiors.

## Visual quality gate

- Can a viewer identify the normal setting?
- Can the viewer point to the abnormal element?
- Is the abnormality readable at phone size?
- Does the frame still work with captions hidden?
- Is the flip adding rather than translating the image?
- Is the frame composition distinct from neighboring answers?
