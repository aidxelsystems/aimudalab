# Rhythm, Timing, Effects, and Line Breaks

## Performance rhythm

- Open with accessible beats, then change joke quality clearly.
- Tighten the back half with shorter phrases, repeated grammar, rhyme, callbacks, or three-beat escalation.
- Use silence and title calls as punctuation.
- Do not compress toward 60 seconds when discovery, a slipped pun, or a payoff needs space.
- Avoid three consecutive long lines, identical sentence endings, identical images, or identical SE.

## Image-first timing

For image-led jokes, show only the image for approximately 0.55-0.75 seconds. Then start flip and voice. Do not start narration at the exact image cut.

## Effect grammar

- Use normal pop for setup.
- Use a heavy impact for major reveals and title calls.
- Use a sharp tsukkomi effect after the spoken objection, not on flip entry.
- Use `チーン`, `ガーン`, or a foolish pause after a spoken pun, then hold the same image briefly.
- Use callback/recovery accents when an earlier motif returns.
- Avoid the same reaction sound three answers in a row and keep sharp tsukkomi beats scarce.

## Answer line breaks

- Prefer 2-3 lines; allow more only for unusually dense text or the outro.
- Aim roughly for 8-15 Japanese characters per line when natural.
- Break at meaning units, not raw character count.
- Put setup/context first and preserve the punch or twist for the final line.
- Keep short quoted phrases together.
- Avoid orphan particles such as `は`, `が`, `を`, `に`, `で`, `と`, `も`, `から` at line starts.
- Keep numerals and counters together.
- Keep `text` for display and `reading` for narration; do not insert display line breaks into readings.

## Title and outro

Use two title lines when the subject fits. Use three lines rather than splitting a compound subject such as `ラーメン屋`.

Separate the final comment invitation and subscription call with a blank line when dense. Give `チャンネル登録も` and `よろしく` separate visual beats.
